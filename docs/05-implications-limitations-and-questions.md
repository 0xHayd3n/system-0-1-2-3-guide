# Implications, limitations, and research questions

The discussion in **arXiv:2503.06138v3**, dated 16 July 2025, asks what follows if embodiment, individual inference, and collective symbol formation are studied together. Its applications are a research agenda. The paper reports no new end-to-end validation of the four-system framework. This module separates the authors' proposals, the earlier evidence they cite, and questions developed by this companion.

## Culture and language: transmission plus grounding

Section 5.1 contrasts the iterated learning tradition with the authors' preferred emphasis on embodied symbol emergence. Iterated learning models examine how expressions change as one generation's productions become another's learning material. The paper credits this literature with explaining aspects of compositionality through simulations, mathematical models, and laboratory studies. Its criticism is that intergenerational transmission alone does not adequately capture the mutual adaptation of expressions, environmental interaction, and internal representations. [§5.1, PDF p. 27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06138v3#page=27)

Generative emergent communication adds representation learning through generative models to the study of shared symbols. Within the CPC interpretation, symbols do more than transmit a finished thought: they participate in building a collective account of the world. This is a proposed connection between research programs. It should not be read as a demonstration that every language or cultural practice minimizes the specific objective in §3.2. [§5.1, PDF pp. 27–28](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06138v3#page=27)

The paper also cites narrower supporting work. It describes Metropolis–Hastings naming-game studies and reports that Okumura et al. (2023) found human name accept/reject decisions better predicted by an MH-based probability than by the compared heuristics. That is the authors' account of an earlier study, not a new experiment in this paper or a result reproduced by this repository. Support for a proposed naming mechanism does not validate the entire quad-process architecture. [§5.1, PDF pp. 27–28](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06138v3#page=27)

## Creativity includes social evaluation

The authors extend their argument to art and entertainment. Generating an artifact is only part of a creative process: evaluators, embodied feelings, community standards, and historical context affect whether it is accepted and how future work develops. They connect this systems perspective to prior creativity research and suggest that interoceptive observations could broaden CPC beyond externally perceived objects. These are proposed modeling directions. A working mechanism integrating generation, evaluation, and emotion remains a challenge. [§5.1, PDF p. 28](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06138v3#page=28)

## Two predictions the authors actually propose

The final paragraphs of §5.1 make the framework more concrete with two future comparisons:

| Author-proposed prediction | Suggested comparison | What remains to be established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Embodiment helps shape a group's symbol system. | Groups of robots with different morphologies, such as rigid and soft manipulation. | Whether language differences reliably track sensorimotor affordances after controlling other differences. |
| Semiotic plasticity improves collective adaptation after environmental change. | Agents allowed to renegotiate language versus agents whose language is fixed. | Whether the adaptive-language condition improves adaptation, and under which conditions. |

These experiments are proposed in v3; they are not reported results. The authors suggest measuring adaptation speed for the second comparison. The companion's control questions in the last column explain what a careful study would need to resolve. [§5.1, PDF p. 28](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06138v3#page=28)

## Consciousness requires careful wording

The discussion connects temporal organization, language, unconscious processes, and qualia. It invokes traditions including Jung and Lacan and points to related CPC work on qualia structure. The stated status is exploratory: how social language and internal representations affect consciousness remains open. [§5.2, PDF p. 29](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06138v3#page=29)

Read this alongside the clarification in §3.3. The contrast between automatic and deliberative **information processing** is not identical to a contrast between absence and presence of **subjective experience**. The paper does not restrict phenomenal consciousness to System 2. Its possible collective or “supra-conscious” phenomenon is explicitly hypothetical. Communication between agents, a shared vocabulary, or lower collective free energy does not by itself establish a conscious collective subject. [§3.3, PDF pp. 20–21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06138v3#page=20)

## Robotics and collective world models

Section 5.3 places the framework beside robotics foundation models and vision-language-action controllers. Passive dynamics make bodily design relevant to control, while socially formed language can guide action. The authors argue that robots with different bodies may need linguistic structures reflecting their own affordances. They also propose treating language itself as a collective world model, since it preserves community experience and knowledge. [§5.3, PDF pp. 29–30](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06138v3#page=29)

This is a useful design perspective, but the paper does not provide a complete robot implementation, an ablation showing the value of four separate systems, or a benchmark comparing this framework with competing integrated architectures. A language-conditioned policy illustrates one connection among components; it does not establish that the policy implements the entire theoretical picture.

## What the conclusion leaves open

Section 6 explicitly calls for empirical validation in embodied AI and human cognition, methods to quantify Systems 0 and 3, and computational architectures combining reactive, deliberative, and collective processes. It also identifies meaning negotiation and changing symbol systems as important for human–AI interaction and future social integration. These are directions for further interdisciplinary work, rather than demonstrated alignment or deployment benefits. [§6, PDF pp. 30–31](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06138v3#page=30)

The following critical points are **companion analysis**:

- **Operational boundaries:** qualitative labels such as fast and super-slow do not yet specify measurements that uniquely assign a process to a system.
- **Level of explanation:** a unifying vocabulary, a mathematical CPC model, and a working architecture make different evidential commitments. Their connections need explicit tests.
- **Competing explanations:** improved coordination could arise from additional computation, memory, or communication without requiring symbol-system adaptation.
- **Landscape interpretation:** extending canalization from physical regularities to culture is philosophically productive but does not establish a shared quantitative law.
- **Collective benefit:** a coordinated group need not improve every member's welfare. The framework does not establish that stable shared symbols are truthful, fair, or beneficial in every context.

## Questions for a reading group or future study

These are **companion proposals**, not experiments performed here:

1. In the morphology comparison, how would you match observations, task difficulty, learning budget, and communication opportunities?
2. In the plastic-language comparison, how would you distinguish renegotiating meaning from simply adding memory or messages?
3. Which measurements would show a changed shared representation rather than changed individual policies alone?
4. What outcome would count against the proposed adaptation advantage, and could fixed conventions outperform flexible ones in a stable environment?
5. What independent evidence would be needed before discussing group phenomenal consciousness?
